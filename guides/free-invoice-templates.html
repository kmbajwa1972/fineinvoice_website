What every invoice template should contain

  • The words "Invoice" and a unique, sequential invoice number
  • Your name or business name and contact details, and the client's name
  • Issue date and a clear due date
  • Itemised lines: description, quantity, unit price, and the line amount
  • Subtotal, any discount, any tax, and the total due, with the currency named
  • How to pay you, and your payment terms

Some countries require extra details, such as a tax registration number or specific wording. Check your local tax authority for the rules that apply to you.

Word, Excel or PDF?

Word suits an occasional invoice you can type in a few minutes. Excel is better when you want the totals calculated and several line items. PDF is what you should send to the client: it cannot be changed by accident and looks the same everywhere. Fill in a template, then export or print to PDF before sending.

Common template mistakes

  • Reusing an old invoice and forgetting to change the number or date
  • Skipping the due date, so the client decides when to pay
  • Typing totals by hand and making an arithmetic slip
  • Leaving out payment details, which forces a back-and-forth email

Frequently asked questions

Can I use these templates for my business?

Yes. They are free to use, edit, and send to clients. Replace the example row and the placeholders with your own details.

Do I need an invoice number?

Yes. A unique number per invoice makes records and payment follow-up easier, and many tax authorities expect sequential numbering.

When should I move beyond a template?

When you invoice every week, keep re-typing client details, or want to track who has paid, a tool that stores clients and numbers invoices for you saves time.

Templates are a starting point and are not accounting or tax advice.

Worked example: checking an Excel invoice template

Imagine a consultant records 6 hours of work at $45 per hour and a separately agreed travel charge of $30. The service line is 6 × $45 = $270. Adding the travel charge gives a pre-tax subtotal of $300. If a 5% discount applies to the full subtotal, the discount is $15 and the discounted amount is $285. If a 8% tax applies to that discounted base in the example, tax is $22.80 and the total is $307.80. This is an arithmetic illustration, not a recommendation about which expenses are taxable.

How to adapt a template safely

  • Replace every sample name, address, invoice number, date and bank detail before using the file.
  • Keep the original formula cells in the Excel version; test them with a small example before entering a real invoice.
  • Use one currency per invoice unless you clearly show and explain any agreed conversion.
  • Save a clean master copy and make a new copy for each invoice so the previous document is not overwritten.
  • Export the final version to PDF and compare the PDF with the source document before sending.

Template test before your first real invoice

Enter a quantity of 2 and a unit price of 10. The line should show 20 before any tax or discount. Then enter a 10% discount and confirm the discounted base is 18. If you apply a 5% tax to that base in the test, the tax is 0.90 and the total is 18.90. Clear the test values before entering customer information.
