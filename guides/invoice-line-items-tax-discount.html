Invoice Line Items, Tax and Discounts Explained

Generated by FineInvoice.com · Updated October 2026

Line items are where invoices go wrong. A missing unit, a discount applied in the wrong place, or tax calculated on the wrong amount can leave you short or start an argument with a client. This guide shows how each part works, with worked numbers and a calculator you can try.

Anatomy of a line item

Each line should answer four questions: what was it (description), how much (quantity and unit), at what price (unit price), and what does it come to (line amount). Use units your client understands: hours, days, pieces, words, pages, or a fixed project fee.

Quantity × unit price

Three hours at 60.00 is a line amount of 180.00. For a fixed fee, use quantity 1.

Discounts

A discount can be a percentage ("10% off") or a fixed amount, and it can apply to one line or the whole invoice. Show it as its own visible figure rather than quietly lowering the price, so the client sees the value you are giving them.

Tax

If every item is taxed at one rate, a single tax figure on the invoice total is enough. If some items are taxable and others are not, or rates differ, put a tax percentage on each line. Mixed rates are common for a combination of services and goods.

Order of calculation

A common method, and the one used in the examples here, is: line amount = quantity × unit price, then subtract the discount, then add tax on the discounted amount. Applying tax before the discount gives a different total, so keep the same method on every invoice and check the rule for your country, since tax authorities can treat discounts differently. Keep the exact decimals

Keep the exact amount, including the cents, and show two decimal places on every figure so the invoice reads clearly: 437.40, not 437. Use the same number of decimals throughout.

Common mistakes

  • Applying tax to the original price when the invoice says the discount comes first
  • Charging tax on items that are exempt, or forgetting it on items that are not
  • Using vague units such as "service" instead of hours, days, or pieces
  • Changing the calculation method between invoices for the same client

Frequently asked questions

Should the discount be per line or on the whole invoice?

Per line when the discount applies to certain items only; on the whole invoice for a general loyalty or early-payment discount.

Do I add tax to every line?

Only to lines that are taxable. If everything is taxable at one rate, one tax figure on the total is simpler.

Is a fixed amount discount handled differently?

You subtract the fixed amount from the line (or subtotal) and then calculate tax on what is left, using the same order as above.

Tax rules vary by country and by item. This is general information, not tax advice.
