How to Follow Up on an Unpaid Invoice Professionally

An unpaid invoice does not always mean a customer is refusing to pay. The invoice may have been missed, sent to the wrong contact, held up by an approval process, or delayed by a question about the work. A calm, clear follow-up gives the customer a chance to resolve the issue while helping you keep your own cash flow and records organized.

The best approach is to follow the payment terms you agreed, check the facts before contacting the customer, and keep each message courteous and specific. The timing examples and email templates below are starting points, not universal rules; adapt them to your contract, customer relationship, and any requirements that apply to your business.

1. Check the invoice before sending a reminder

Take a moment to confirm that the invoice is genuinely overdue and that the details are correct. An avoidable error can delay payment and make an otherwise simple reminder uncomfortable.

  • Confirm the invoice number, issue date, amount, currency, and agreed due date.
  • Check that the invoice was sent to the correct contact and any required accounts-payable address or portal.
  • Review the contract, purchase order, or email agreement for approval steps and payment terms.
  • Check your bank account or payment processor for a payment that has arrived but has not yet been matched to the invoice.
  • Make sure any agreed deposit, credit, discount, or previous partial payment is reflected in the balance.
  • Gather supporting details for any line item the customer might reasonably ask about.

If the customer must approve a deliverable before payment becomes due, verify when that approval happened. If the invoice was submitted incorrectly or the due date was calculated using the wrong starting point, correct the record first rather than sending an inaccurate demand.

2. Choose a reasonable follow-up schedule

A useful schedule is predictable without becoming aggressive. The right interval depends on the terms, the customer's normal payment cycle, and whether they have already replied.

  • Before or on the due date: If appropriate, send a friendly courtesy reminder with the invoice attached and the payment instructions.
  • Shortly after the due date: Send a brief check-in asking whether the invoice was received and when payment is expected.
  • After a further delay: Refer to the earlier message, restate the amount and due date, and request a clear update or payment date.
  • When reminders go unanswered: Review your agreement and records, then decide on a proportionate next step. Communicate any formal notice in the manner required by your contract or local rules.

For example, a freelancer might check in a few business days after a missed due date, then follow up again after another week if there is no response. A large organization may have a documented accounts-payable cycle that requires a different approach. Avoid sending repeated messages every day; allow a reasonable time for the customer to respond.

3. Template: a friendly first reminder

Use a neutral tone and assume the delay may be an oversight. Include enough information for the customer to identify the invoice without searching through old messages.

Subject: Friendly reminder — Invoice [invoice number]

Hello [Customer Name],

I hope you’re well. I’m checking in about invoice [invoice number] for [amount], dated [invoice date] and due on [due date]. I’ve attached a copy for convenience.

Could you let me know whether it has reached the right person and when payment is expected? If you need any details from me, I’ll be happy to help.

Thank you,
[Your Name]

4. Template: the invoice is overdue

If the due date has passed and you have not received a response, be more direct while remaining professional. State the agreed due date and ask for a specific update.

Subject: Follow-up on overdue invoice [invoice number]

Hello [Customer Name],

I’m following up on invoice [invoice number] for [amount], which was due on [due date]. Our records show that the balance is still outstanding. I’ve attached the invoice and included the payment details below for reference.

Please confirm the expected payment date, or let me know if there is an issue or question we should resolve.

Thank you for your attention,
[Your Name]

Only say that your records show the invoice is unpaid after checking for recent payments. If the customer has already arranged a transfer, ask for the payment reference or expected clearing date rather than assuming they have ignored the invoice.

5. Template: a further follow-up or final notice

If earlier messages have not produced an answer, summarize the history and make your request clear. Before calling a message a “final notice,” check whether your contract or local rules prescribe specific wording, delivery methods, or notice periods.

Subject: Action requested — Invoice [invoice number]

Hello [Customer Name],

I’m writing again about invoice [invoice number] for [amount], due on [due date]. I previously followed up on [dates], but I have not yet received payment or an update.

Please contact me by [reasonable response date] to confirm the payment date or explain any issue that needs to be resolved. If payment has already been sent, please share the transaction reference so I can check our records.

I’d appreciate your reply so we can agree on the next step.

Regards,
[Your Name]

Set a response date that is reasonable for the circumstances and consistent with any applicable notice requirements. Do not threaten court action, add fees, or promise consequences unless you have checked the agreement and the rules that apply.

6. If the customer says they already paid

Thank them for the update and ask for the payment date, amount, and transaction reference if you need those details to locate it. Check for differences in the payer name, transfer fees, currency, or processing time. Once the payment is confirmed, update the invoice status and send a receipt or confirmation if that is part of your process.

Do not send more overdue reminders while you are checking a plausible payment claim. If the amount received is lower than expected, compare it with the agreed terms and any fees or deductions that were authorized before asking the customer to pay a remaining balance.

7. If the customer disputes the invoice

Ask which line item or part of the work they believe is incorrect. Compare the concern with your quote, contract, approved changes, delivery records, and messages. Respond to the specific issue rather than arguing about the entire invoice.

If an error is yours, explain how you will correct it and issue the appropriate revised document. If only part of the invoice is disputed, record the disputed amount and discuss whether the undisputed portion can be paid under the agreement. The correct treatment depends on your contract and local requirements; do not silently change or delete an issued invoice to make the disagreement disappear.

8. Keep a simple follow-up record

A small tracking sheet or invoicing system can prevent missed promises and duplicate reminders. For each outstanding invoice, record the invoice number, customer, amount, due date, last contact date, response, promised payment date, and next action. Keep copies of messages and relevant supporting documents in an appropriate place with access limited to people who need them.

After a customer promises to pay on a certain date, note that date and follow up if the payment does not arrive. If the customer asks for a copy of the invoice, send the original or a clearly identified copy so they can match it to their internal records.

9. Prevent repeat delays on future invoices

Prevention starts before the invoice is sent. Agree on payment terms and the correct billing contact at the start of the job. For larger projects, define deposits, milestones, approval steps, and how scope changes will be priced. Send invoices promptly after the agreed trigger and make the amount, due date, currency, and payment instructions easy to find.

  • Use a consistent invoice number and clear file name.
  • State the agreed due date rather than relying only on vague wording.
  • Include the customer's required purchase-order number or submission details.
  • Check line items, tax calculations, credits, and the final balance.
  • Keep payment terms consistent with the accepted proposal or contract.

Late fees or interest should never be added automatically just because an invoice is overdue. Check that the charge was properly agreed, is permitted, and is calculated in the required way before applying it.

Keep it professional: a reminder should make it easy to identify the invoice and resolve the next step. Clear facts, a courteous tone, and a record of contact are more useful than blame or repeated urgent messages.
