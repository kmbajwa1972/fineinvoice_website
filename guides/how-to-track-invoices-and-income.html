How to Track Your Invoices and Income

Generated by FineInvoice.com · Updated October 2026

Sending an invoice is only half the job. The other half is knowing which invoices have been paid, which are late, and how much money you are still waiting for. A simple tracking habit stops invoices from slipping through the cracks and gives you a clear picture of your income.

What to record for every invoice

  • Invoice number and client name
  • Issue date and due date
  • Invoice amount and the amount received so far
  • Balance still owed
  • Status: Sent, Part paid, Paid or Overdue

Eight columns are enough. The skill is keeping them up to date, ideally the same day a payment arrives.

What each status means

StatusMeaningWhat to do
SentIssued, not yet due, nothing paidNothing, unless the due date is close
Part paidSome money received, balance remainsRecord the payment and confirm when the rest is due
PaidBalance is zeroSend a receipt if the client needs one
OverdueDue date passed and a balance remainsSend a reminder (see the follow-up guide)

Group late invoices by age

Sorting unpaid invoices by how late they are tells you where to focus: current (not due yet), 1 to 30 days late (a friendly reminder), 31 to 60 days late (a firmer message and a phone call), and over 60 days (a final notice and a decision about further steps). The older an invoice gets, the harder it usually is to collect.

A simple routine

  • Same day: record every payment you receive.
  • Weekly: check for invoices that became overdue and send reminders.
  • Monthly: compare invoiced and received amounts and look at income by client.

Numbers worth watching

  • Outstanding balance: money you are owed right now.
  • Overdue amount: the part of that already past due.
  • Average days to get paid: add up the days between issue date and payment for each paid invoice and divide by how many there are. If it is much longer than your payment terms, your terms or follow-up need work.
  • Income by client: if one client is most of your income, losing them would hurt, so it is worth knowing.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting to record part payments, so the balance looks wrong
  • Tracking invoices in several places that disagree
  • Only looking at the list when money is tight
  • Chasing a client for an invoice that was actually paid

Frequently asked questions

Do I need accounting software?

For a handful of invoices a month, a spreadsheet works. Accounting software helps when you need tax reporting, bank matching or several users.

Should I track quotes and draft invoices too?

Only if it helps you. The tracker is meant for invoices that have actually been sent.

How long should I keep invoice records?

Record-keeping periods are set by tax rules and differ by country, so check your tax authority.

This guide is general information about keeping your own records. It is not accounting or tax advice.
